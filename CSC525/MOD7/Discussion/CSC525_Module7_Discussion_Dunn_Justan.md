## Prompt Review

The Module 7 discussion asks students to reflect on supervised and unsupervised
learning, generative AI, and the question of "true AI." The visible peer posts
mostly focused on whether ChatGPT or generative AI counts as true AI. Several
also discussed whether supervised learning can lead to self-awareness. The
least-used topic was the second prompt: whether constrained, safety-controlled
AI can still be considered genuine artificial intelligence.

## Initial Post

I think an AI system can be constrained for safety and still be considered a
genuine form of artificial intelligence, but that does not automatically mean it
has self-awareness or true autonomy. Constraints are not the same thing as a
lack of intelligence. Humans operate within laws, social rules, professional
codes, and physical limits, but those constraints do not erase human
intelligence. In the same way, an AI system could be intelligent in a functional
sense if it can learn from data, generalize to new situations, reason across
different inputs, and adapt its outputs to a goal. The harder question is
whether that system has internally generated goals, grounded understanding, and
self-directed agency. Current generative AI models still depend heavily on
training data, model architecture, human feedback, and safety rules. They can
produce useful and sometimes impressive responses, but they do not appear to
possess personal experience, independent motivation, or awareness of what their
outputs mean.

For that reason, I would separate "genuine AI" from "true autonomous
consciousness." A constrained AI system can still be genuine AI if it performs
adaptive cognitive work that would normally require intelligence, such as
language understanding, planning support, classification, or decision
recommendation. However, it remains a tool unless it can form goals, evaluate
its own beliefs, and revise its behavior for reasons that are not simply
provided by human-defined objectives. This distinction matters because safety
constraints are not only limits; they are part of responsible AI design. Russell
(2019) argues that advanced AI should be built around uncertainty about human
preferences rather than unchecked optimization. In that view, control and
alignment do not make AI less real. They make it safer. At the same time, Bender
et al. (2021) warn that language models can produce fluent text without grounded
understanding. So my position is that constrained AI can be genuine AI as an
engineered cognitive system, but today's generative models are better described
as powerful language and pattern systems rather than self-aware autonomous
intelligence.

References

Bender, E. M., Gebru, T., McMillan-Major, A., & Shmitchell, S. (2021). On the
dangers of stochastic parrots: Can language models be too big? Proceedings of

https://doi.org/10.1145/3442188.3445922

Russell, S. (2019). Human compatible: Artificial intelligence and the problem of

## Selected Peer Reply 1: Brennan Derrick

Brennan, your post raises one of the more difficult parts of this discussion:
whether there is any empirical test that can separate real understanding from
high-quality approximation. I think that is where purely behavioral tests become
limited. If a model can produce a convincing answer, the behavior may look like
understanding even if the internal process is still statistical prediction. A
stronger test might need several parts: grounded interaction with the outside
world, the ability to transfer knowledge to counterfactual situations, calibrated
uncertainty when it does not know something, and some kind of persistent
self-model or goal state. Even then, that would still show functional
understanding more than consciousness. Your distinction between self-supervised
pretraining and later human feedback is also important because it shows that
modern generative AI is not simply classic supervised learning. However, I still
think the human feedback stage matters because it shapes what the model is
rewarded for presenting as useful or safe. That makes the system powerful, but
still externally directed.

## Selected Peer Reply 2: Shourav Mandal

Shourav, I think your "middle ground" framing is a useful way to avoid making
the debate too binary. Calling generative AI "just a pattern matcher" can
undersell what happens when very large models begin to combine concepts, write
code, summarize arguments, and reason across domains. At the same time, I agree
with your point that this still does not make it a mind. One distinction that
helps me is the difference between apparent reasoning and self-directed agency.
A model can generate a response that looks reasoned, but it does not necessarily
care about the result, remember the consequence by default, or choose its own
long-term objective. That is why I think the final question is less about
whether pattern recognition can look intelligent and more about whether the
system can build stable goals and revise its own model of the world in a
grounded way. Until that happens, generative AI seems like an advanced cognitive
tool rather than autonomous intelligence.
